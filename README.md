# Exploratory Data Analysis of the Titanic Disaster

**CodeAlpha Data Analytics Internship - Task: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)**

A complete, reproducible EDA of the Titanic passenger dataset (891 passengers, 12 variables) that answers one
question: **who survived, and why?** The analysis follows the five required EDA steps and ends with an analysis-ready
dataset and a documented list of data issues.

![Survival overview](outputs/figures/03_survival_overview.png)

## Task requirements -> where they are covered

| Requirement | Where in the notebook | Key output |
|---|---|---|
| Ask meaningful questions before analysis | Section 1 | 7 guiding questions, 6 formal hypotheses (H1-H6), assumptions to validate |
| Explore data structure, variables and data types | Section 2 | Data dictionary, measurement levels, summary statistics, missing-value chart |
| Identify trends, patterns and anomalies | Section 3 | Univariate, bivariate and interaction plots, correlation matrix |

| Detect data issues for further analysis | Section 5 | 12-item issue log, outlier analysis, cleaning pipeline, `titanic_clean.csv` |

## Key findings

* **38.4%** of passengers survived (342 of 891).
* **Sex is the dominant factor:** 74.2% of women vs 18.9% of men survived (Cramer's V = 0.54, a large effect).
* **Class matters:** survival was 63.0% / 47.3% / 24.2% in 1st / 2nd / 3rd class, and it **interacts with sex** -
  3rd-class women survived only 50%, against 92-97% in the upper classes.
* **Family size is non-linear:** travelling alone 30.4%, small family (2-4) 57.9%, large family (5+) 16.1%.
* **Age alone is a weak predictor** (rank-biserial r = -0.06); the effect is concentrated in young children (58% survival).
* **Port of embarkation is partly confounded with class:** Cherbourg had 50.6% first-class passengers; the gap shrinks within
  first class but persists in third class.
* **Hypothesis tests:** 5 of 6 hypotheses are rejected after Bonferroni correction; age is the exception.
  Test choice was driven by assumption checks (Fare is highly skewed, so rank-based tests were used).

![Effect sizes](outputs/figures/10_effect_sizes.png)

### Data issues found (full log in `outputs/data_issues_log.csv`)
* `Age` 19.9% missing and **not random** (more missing in 3rd class; survival differs) -> group-wise imputation + missing flag
* `Cabin` 77.1% missing and informative -> engineered `HasCabin` and `Deck`
* `Fare` extremely skewed (4.79), 116 IQR outliers, 15 suspicious zero fares -> log transform, flagged not removed
* 344 passengers share tickets, so observations are **not independent** -> group-aware cross-validation recommended
* Multicollinearity (`FamilySize` vs `SibSp`/`Parch`) and mis-typed variables (`Survived`, `Pclass`, `PassengerId`)

## Methods and tools
Python 3, pandas, NumPy, SciPy (chi-square, Mann-Whitney U, Welch t-test, Levene, D'Agostino-Pearson, Kruskal-Wallis, Spearman),
Matplotlib, Seaborn. Effect sizes: Cramer's V, rank-biserial correlation, Cohen's d. Multiple testing: Bonferroni.

## Limitations
Observational data (associations, not causes); 891 of roughly 2,200 people aboard; passengers who travelled together are not
independent, so p-values are somewhat optimistic; 20% of ages are imputed; zero fares and missing cabins cannot be verified.
